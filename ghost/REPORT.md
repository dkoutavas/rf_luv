# How "paranormal investigation" equipment works

A mechanism-level write-up. Every effect a spirit box, an EMF meter or a
Bluetooth speaker produces in a "ghost hunt" has a known, reproducible cause.
This report builds those causes from first principles on an SDR station in
Athens, Greece, and tests them against published video.

The write-up is about the equipment. It names no channel and no person.

---

## 1. The spirit box

### What it is

A spirit box (SB7, SB11, and their clones) is an FM/AM radio receiver that
sweeps broadcast stations at a fixed rate — typically 100 to 350 ms per step —
with no squelch and no frequency lock. It dumps the raw demodulated audio from
every step into a single stream.

Words in that stream come from broadcast fragments. The receiver lands on a
station for a fraction of a second, picks up whatever syllable is on air, and
moves on. Meaning comes from the listener.

### Replication

`ghost/spiritbox.py` rebuilds this receiver on an RTL-SDR V3 dongle (rtl_tcp,
87.5–108 MHz, 240 kHz capture rate per step, 150 ms dwell). The demodulation
chain is a quadrature discriminator, a 50 µs de-emphasis filter (EU standard),
an anti-alias low-pass at 15 kHz, and decimation to 48 kHz — the same FM
broadcast receiver path any radio uses. The output is a WAV file.

Before the sweep, the tool dwells 4 s on each strong FM carrier and decodes its
RDS data (Programme Service name, Programme Identification code, RadioText).
That labels every fragment in the WAV with the station it came from: frequency,
name, and timestamp.

Polygono, Athens, 2026-09-19. Two sessions, 206 steps each, gain 12. Stations
decoded:

| MHz   | PI     | PS         | RadioText excerpt       |
|-------|--------|------------|-------------------------|
| 102.2 | 0x6560 |            | L.21                    |
| 105.8 | 0x1201 | TEL 8 FM   | 105,8 MHz &             |
| 105.8 | 0x1201 | 105.8 FM   | ertecho.gr/radio/...    |
| 105.9 | 0x1201 |            | 91,6 MHz ATH            |
| 106.2 | 0x3D44 |            |                         |
| 107.3 | 0x10DF |            |                         |

Every "word" in the sweep WAV is a fragment of one of these broadcasts. The
sidecar JSON ties each step to its source frequency and station name.

### The "creepy voice" effect

The raw sweep sounds like choppy radio. Add a single slapback delay (one echo
tap at 60–120 ms, feedback 0.2–0.3) and a short reverb, and it sounds like a
voice speaking from inside a room. This is a standard audio production effect —
one knob on a delay plugin.

The repository includes a dry WAV and an `_fx` WAV (slapback 90 ms / feedback
0.3 + reverb decay 0.35) from the same sweep, generated by `ghost/dsp.py`. They
are the same audio. The difference is one delay tap.

## 2. The EMF meter

### What it is

A K-II (and its clones) is a single-axis AC magnetic field detector with five
LEDs and a momentary-contact button. It costs about 20 EUR. It responds to:

- **Phone bursts.** GSM TDMA at 217 Hz. A phone negotiating a cell connection
  within 1 m lights the meter reliably.
- **PMR446 handhelds.** A walkie-talkie keyed nearby produces a strong RF burst
  at 446 MHz that couples into the meter's unshielded input.
- **Mains wiring.** 50 Hz AC fields from in-wall cables, especially near
  junction boxes and older wiring.
- **The camera itself.** An active camera with wireless (Wi-Fi, Bluetooth)
  transmits continuously.

The meter does not distinguish between these sources. It reports field strength
on a 5-LED bar with no frequency information.

### The button

The K-II uses a momentary-contact power button, not a latching switch. Pressure
on the button is what keeps it on. Intermittent finger pressure — resting a
thumb on it loosely — causes the LEDs to flicker with no external field at all.
This is a mechanical artefact of the switch, not a reading.

### RF correlation

`ghost/forensics/emf_sync.py` lines up logged EMF-LED timestamps from video
with impulsive audio bursts (PTT clicks from a keyed radio) and the 217 Hz GSM
buzz envelope. A coincidence between an EMF event and an RF burst means the
meter responded to a transmitter, not to an unexplained field.

The SDR can confirm the transmitter fired: park one dongle on 446.0–446.2 MHz
and log the PMR446 burst on the same timeline as the LED response. That is the
clean correlation: RF event on the SDR, "spirit" on the meter.

## 3. The speaker

### What it is

A portable Bluetooth speaker (JBL, Marshall, or similar) paired to a phone and
playing pre-recorded audio. In a dark room the speaker is not visible. The
"voice" comes from the speaker, not from the room.

### Detection from audio

Bluetooth audio (SBC or AAC codec over A2DP) imposes a hard high-frequency
roll-off that live room audio through the camera microphone does not have.
`ghost/forensics/bandlimit.py` measures the effective upper bandwidth of a
segment: a hard shelf well below the Nyquist that the surrounding room tone
lacks points to a rendered source, not a live one.

`ghost/forensics/delay_estimate.py` measures the slapback delay on "voice"
segments by autocorrelation with a cepstrum cross-check. A tap that is the same
across episodes — same millisecond value, same feedback — is a plugin preset,
not a property of a room.

`ghost/forensics/fingerprint.py` computes chromaprint acoustic fingerprints
across all flagged segments. A near-identical fingerprint in two different
episodes means the same audio clip was played twice. That is the strongest
evidence of pre-recorded material.

`ghost/forensics/reverb_match.py` estimates the RT60 of the room from claps or
door slams in the footage, and compares it with the reverb tail on the "voice".
A tail that does not match the room was added in processing or in the speaker's
DSP, not picked up from the room.

## 4. Why it works: the perception layer

A spirit box session is structured to produce the perception of meaningful
communication:

1. **Priming.** The operator says "is anyone here?" and then plays the sweep.
   The listener now expects an answer. Any phoneme that sounds like "yes" is
   heard as "yes".

2. **Apophenia.** The human auditory system finds speech patterns in noise. FM
   broadcast fragments contain real speech in Greek, English, and other
   languages. At 150 ms per step, fragments are too short to understand as
   broadcast, but long enough to trigger word recognition.

3. **Confirmation bias.** Hits are remembered; misses are not. A session with
   200 steps and one fragment that sounds like a word is remembered as "it said
   something". The other 199 fragments are forgotten.

4. **The dark room.** Reduced visual input raises auditory attention and lowers
   the threshold for perceiving patterns in ambiguous sounds. The same audio
   played in daylight with the mechanism explained does not produce the same
   perception.

`ghost/blindtest/` (planned) tests this directly: 20 sweep fragments, half with
a suggested "answer" shown before playback, half without. The primed group
reports hearing the suggested word more often. That is the effect, measured.

## Method

All reception is legal in Greece (EU framework). No encrypted content is
decoded. RDS is public broadcast metadata. TETRA and digital voice are
explicitly excluded. The EEPROM serial scheme and the coordinator lock prevent
accidental interference between dongles. No transmission is made. No session of
any person is attended, recorded, or interfered with. Every input is either
self-generated audio in the flat or a publicly available video.

The SDR station, the demodulation code, the forensic tools, and this report are
open source at [github.com/dkoutavas/rf_luv](https://github.com/dkoutavas/rf_luv)
(`ghost/` directory).
